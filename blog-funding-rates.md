# 作品集样例 #2：英文博客（800 词）

**标题：Funding Rates: The Invisible Hand Moving Billions in Crypto**

*说明：投 content/writing 岗的 writing sample。主题选永续合约资金费率——DeFi 硬核概念，写给普通读者，展示把复杂讲简单的能力。*

---

Every eight hours, billions of dollars change hands in crypto — and most traders never notice. No headlines, no candlesticks, no drama. Just a quiet transfer from one group of traders to another, executed automatically by the exchange.

It's called the funding rate. And if you trade perpetual futures without understanding it, you're paying a tax you can't see.

## What funding actually is

Perpetual futures ("perps") are crypto's most popular derivative. Unlike traditional futures, they never expire. That creates a problem: without an expiry date forcing convergence, what keeps the perp price tethered to the real spot price?

The answer is funding — a periodic payment between longs and shorts.

When the perp trades **above** spot (too many bulls), longs pay shorts. When it trades **below** spot (too many bears), shorts pay longs. The payment drags the price back toward spot like a rubber band. Most major exchanges settle it every 8 hours.

Think of it as rent. If you're on the crowded side of the boat, you pay the people keeping it balanced.

## Positive vs. negative: who pays whom

- **Positive funding** (+0.01% per 8h is typical): longs pay shorts. The market is bullish and leveraged longs are the crowded trade.
- **Negative funding** (−0.01%): shorts pay longs. Bearish crowded.

These numbers look tiny. They aren't. A +0.05% rate per 8-hour period compounds to roughly **55% annualized** — paid in three daily installments, win or lose on your actual position. Hold a leveraged long through a euphoric week and funding alone can eat your margin.

This is why funding is sentiment in its purest form. Price tells you where the market *is*. Funding tells you how *desperate* everyone is to stay positioned.

## Why smart money watches funding, not price

Three things funding reveals that price hides:

**1. Crowding.** Extremely positive funding means everyone's already long. There's nobody left to buy — the classic setup for a long squeeze. Some of the sharpest reversal signals in crypto history printed first in funding, not price.

**2. The carry trade.** When funding is deeply positive, traders run "delta-neutral" strategies: long spot, short perps, collecting funding while hedged against price moves. It's one of the few genuinely market-neutral yields in crypto — when it works.

**3. Regime changes.** Funding flipping from positive to negative often marks trend exhaustion before price confirms. It's an early-warning radar for anyone positioned directionally.

## The catch nobody mentions

Funding-based strategies look like free money on a spreadsheet. In practice, three things kill them:

- **Capital efficiency.** Exchanges demand margin on both legs. Your "20% APY" might be 20% on half your capital.
- **Execution.** Spot and perp legs must be opened near-simultaneously. Slippage and fees compound across rebalances.
- **Tail risk.** Funding can spike 10x in minutes during liquidations. Models built on average rates blow up on the extremes.

I learned this watching a scanner flag "profitable" funding spreads all day — then checking the order books and finding nothing executable. The screen shows theory. The book shows reality.

## How to read it yourself

Every major exchange publishes live funding rates: Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid all have public dashboards. What to look for:

- **Sustained extremes** (>0.1% per 8h) in either direction — crowded, fragile.
- **Divergence** — price making new highs while funding fades means momentum without conviction.
- **Flips** — funding crossing zero after a long stretch often front-runs trend changes by hours or days.

You don't need to trade on it. Just add it to your morning check alongside price. Within a week, you'll start seeing the market in a second dimension.

## The bottom line

Price is the argument. Funding is the receipt — proof of who's actually paying to hold their opinion. In a market built on leverage, the people paying rent reveal more than the people shouting predictions.

The invisible hand isn't invisible. It's published every eight hours. Most people just never look.
